Business Closures & Insolvencies in Australia

Australian Business Under Pressure

Formal corporate insolvencies have risen sharply in Australia — but the pressure is not distributed evenly. This visualisation follows the national rise, identifies the industries under the greatest pressure, compares broader business turnover, and shows where insolvencies are concentrated geographically.

01 The national picture

Formal insolvencies rose dramatically

We begin with companies entering external administration for the first time under ASIC Series 1 statistics.

Formal insolvencies climbed from 4,912 in 2021–22 to a peak of 14,722 in 2024–25 before easing slightly in 2025–26.

≠

Formal insolvency is not the same as business exit

ASIC statistics measure companies entering formal external administration. ABS business exits capture a much broader range of reasons why businesses leave the market.

Formal insolvency is therefore only one part of Australia's broader business turnover. ABS entry and exit statistics provide a wider view of how businesses entered and left the market.

ABS business-entry rates remained above exit rates during this period, showing that overall business churn tells a different story from formal corporate insolvency.

02 Industry pressure

The increase was concentrated in particular industries

Looking beneath the national total reveals large differences between industries — and shows that the pressure was far from even.

Key finding

Construction consistently carried the largest formal insolvency burden, with Accommodation and Food Services also heavily affected.

Construction recorded the greatest formal insolvency burden, followed by Accommodation and Food Services.

The top three industries remained remarkably stable, while several middle-ranked industries moved noticeably over time.

Construction contributed the largest individual increase between 2022–23 and 2025–26, with Accommodation and Food Services also adding substantially to the rise.

03 Business turnover

High exit rates do not always mean the most insolvencies

ABS business entry and exit statistics provide a broader view of turnover and reveal a different pattern from formal insolvency.

Transport, Postal and Warehousing recorded the highest business exit rate in 2025–26, illustrating why business exits and formal insolvencies should not be treated as the same measure.

04 Geographic pressure

Where did the most formal insolvencies occur?

The national total also hides substantial geographic differences. Looking across states and territories reveals where the largest absolute numbers of formal insolvencies were recorded in 2025–26.

New South Wales recorded the largest total, followed by Victoria and Queensland.
