Business Closures & Insolvencies in Australia

Australian Business Under Pressure

Formal corporate insolvencies have risen sharply in Australia — but the pressure is not distributed evenly. This visualisation follows the national rise, identifies the industries under the greatest pressure, compares broader business turnover, and shows where insolvencies are concentrated geographically.

01 The national picture

Formal insolvencies rose dramatically

We begin with companies entering external administration or having a controller appointed for the first time under ASIC Series 1 statistics.

CHART 01

The annual rise, and the months behind it

ASIC Series 1 · annual totals for 2021–22 to 2025–26

Five complete financial years. Select monthly detail to inspect shorter fluctuations.

Insolvencies nearly tripled between 2021–22 and 2024–25, then fell 3.9% in 2025–26. The monthly view covers July 2019 to August 2026; July–August 2026 are provisional. Monthly counts are not seasonally adjusted, so individual spikes do not establish a seasonal pattern.

≠

Formal insolvency is not the same as business exit

ASIC Series 1 counts the first external administration or controller appointment for a company. ABS business exits capture a much broader range of reasons why businesses leave the market.

Formal insolvency is therefore only one part of Australia's broader business turnover. ABS entry and exit statistics provide a wider view of how businesses entered and left the market.

CHART 02

Business entries stayed ahead of exits

ABS · entry and exit rates, 2022–23 to 2025–26

In 2025–26, 460,461 businesses entered and 375,331 exited: a net gain of 85,130. The published entry and exit rates differ by 3.1 percentage points. Both use businesses operating at the start of the year as their denominator.

02 Industry pressure

The increase was concentrated in particular industries

Looking beneath the national total reveals large differences between industries — and shows that the pressure was far from even.

Key finding

Construction consistently recorded the largest number of formal insolvencies, followed by Accommodation and Food Services. These totals do not measure an individual business’s risk.

CHART 03

Which industries recorded the most insolvencies?

Counts, not risk · ranked by 2025–26 totals

The default view shows the ten largest industries by 2025–26 insolvency counts; select “All industries” for all 19 named industries. Every cell uses the same colour scale and shows its exact count. Unknown industry is excluded here (36 companies in 2025–26), but included in the national total and waterfall. Counts show volume, not the likelihood of failure.

CHART 04

Which industries moved up the ranking?

Every industry reaching the top eight in any year · ranked against all 19 named industries

A line follows the same industry through all five years, even after it drops below eighth place. Rank 1 is the highest count; ranks are calculated across all 19 named industries before selecting the displayed group. Construction is highlighted in blue by default; other industries provide grey context. Choose an industry to follow its complete path, or select All for an overview. Equal counts share a rank.

CHART 05

How industry changes add up to the national rise

7,942 in 2022–23 + 6,211 additional insolvencies = 14,153 in 2025–26

Each floating bar adds one industry’s change to the running national total. Dark bars show the starting and ending totals; blue bars show increases. “All remaining industries” includes every omitted industry and Unknown, so the bridge accounts for the full increase of 6,211 (78.2%). Construction contributes 1,259, or 20.3% of that increase.

03 Business turnover

High exit rates do not always mean the most insolvencies

ABS business entry and exit statistics provide a broader view of turnover and reveal a different pattern from formal insolvency.

CHART 06

Business turnover tells a different story

ABS · industry entry and exit rates in 2025–26

Transport, Postal and Warehousing had the highest exit rate at 21.7%, alongside a 26.5% entry rate. Circles show entries and crosses show exits, including when rates overlap. ABS exits include businesses leaving for many reasons; they are not equivalent to ASIC corporate insolvencies.

04 Geographic pressure

Location changes the comparison

The national total also hides substantial geographic differences. Compare insolvencies relative to the business population, then examine the absolute totals below.

CHART 07

Where were insolvencies higher relative to business numbers?

2025–26 · insolvencies per 10,000 active businesses at the start of the year

The ACT has the highest ratio (69.2 per 10,000), while NSW has the largest total. This comparison divides ASIC insolvencies by ABS active businesses at 1 July 2025. ABS includes sole traders, partnerships and other businesses as well as companies; this is a measure relative to business numbers, not a company failure probability. The agencies also use different geographic allocation methods.
