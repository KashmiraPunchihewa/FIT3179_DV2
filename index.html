Business Closures & Insolvencies in Australia

Australian Business Under Pressure

Formal corporate insolvencies have risen sharply in Australia — but the pressure is not distributed evenly. This visualisation follows the national rise, identifies the industries under the greatest pressure, compares broader business turnover, and shows where insolvencies are concentrated geographically.

01 The national picture

Formal insolvencies rose dramatically

We begin with companies entering external administration or having a controller appointed for the first time under ASIC Series 1 statistics.

CHART 01

The annual rise, and the months behind it

ASIC Series 1 · annual totals for 2021–22 to 2025–26

Five complete financial years. Select monthly detail to inspect shorter fluctuations.

Insolvencies nearly tripled between 2021–22 and 2024–25, then fell 3.9% in 2025–26. The monthly view covers July 2019 to September 2026; July–September 2026 are provisional. Monthly counts are not seasonally adjusted, so individual spikes do not establish a seasonal pattern.

≠

Formal insolvency is not the same as business exit

ASIC Series 1 counts the first external administration or controller appointment for a company. ABS business exits capture a much broader range of reasons why businesses leave the market.

CHART 02

Entries added more businesses than exits removed

ABS · 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026 · net gain of 85,130 businesses

Start with 2,729,648 businesses. Add 460,461 entries and subtract 375,331 exits to reach 2,814,778 at year-end. Dark bars show the opening and closing totals; floating bars show additions and removals. These ABS business counts are broader than ASIC corporate insolvencies.

Formal insolvency is therefore only one part of Australia's broader business turnover. ABS entry and exit statistics provide a wider view of how businesses entered and left the market.

CHART 03

Business entries stayed ahead of exits

ABS · entry and exit rates, 2022–23 to 2025–26

In 2025–26, entries exceeded exits by 85,130 businesses. The entry–exit rate gap was 3.1 percentage points. Both rates use businesses operating at the start of the year.

02 Industry pressure

The increase was concentrated in particular industries

Looking beneath the national total reveals large differences between industries — and shows that the pressure was far from even.

Key finding

Construction consistently recorded the largest number of formal insolvencies, followed by Accommodation and Food Services. These totals do not measure an individual business’s risk.

CHART 04

Which industries recorded the most insolvencies?

Counts, not risk · ranked by 2025–26 totals

The default view shows the ten largest industries by 2025–26 insolvency counts; select “All industries” for all 19 named industries. Every cell uses the same colour scale and shows its exact count. Unknown industry is excluded here (36 companies in 2025–26), but included in the national total and industry-contribution chart. Counts show volume, not the likelihood of failure.

CHART 05

Which industries moved up the ranking?

Every industry reaching the top eight in any year · ranked against all 19 named industries

A line follows the same industry through all five years, even after it drops below eighth place. Rank 1 is the highest count; ranks are calculated across all 19 named industries before selecting the displayed group. Construction is highlighted in blue by default; other industries provide grey context. Choose an industry to follow its complete path, or select All for an overview. Equal counts share a rank.

CHART 06

Which industries contributed most to the increase?

National total: 7,942 → 14,153 · increase of 6,211 (78.2%)

Each bar compares 2025–26 with 2022–23 for the same industry. Construction contributed 1,259 additional companies, or 20.3% of the increase. The grey bar combines every remaining industry, including Unknown. The eight bars together account for all 6,211 additional insolvencies.

CHART 07

Two industries accounted for nearly four in ten insolvencies

2025–26 · larger and darker rectangles mean a larger share

Construction and accommodation/food services accounted for 39.2% of insolvencies. Both rectangle area and colour darkness represent share of the national total. Hover or tap for the count and percentage. Shares describe volume, not failure risk.

03 Business turnover

The gap between entries and exits varies by industry

ABS business entry and exit statistics provide a broader view of turnover and reveal a different pattern from formal insolvency.

CHART 08

Where did entry rates outpace exit rates most?

Industries ordered by entry minus exit · 2025–26

Health Care and Social Assistance had the largest positive gap: 6.7 percentage points. Each connecting line shows the gap between entry (circle) and exit (cross). Positive labels mean entries exceeded exits; negative labels mean the reverse. These are ABS business movements, not corporate insolvencies.

CHART 08B

The entry–exit gap widened most in information and media

ABS · selected industry contrasts · 2022–23 versus 2025–26

Information and media had the largest widening among all 19 industries: from 0.4 to 3.9 percentage points. Manufacturing had the largest narrowing, from 0.8 to 0.2. Health care retained the largest gap, at 6.7. These three examples contrast widening, narrowing and a persistently large gap. Lines connect endpoints; they do not show the intervening years. Gaps use published rounded rates, not corporate failure probabilities.

04 Geographic pressure

Location changes the comparison

The national total also hides substantial geographic differences. Compare relative intensity, annual changes and the geographic concentration of companies.

CHART 09

Where were insolvencies higher relative to business numbers?

2025–26 · insolvencies per 10,000 active businesses at the start of the year

The ACT has the highest ratio (69.2 per 10,000), while NSW has the largest total. This comparison divides ASIC insolvencies by ABS active businesses at 1 July 2025. ABS includes sole traders, partnerships and other businesses as well as companies; this is a measure relative to business numbers, not a company failure probability. The agencies also use different geographic allocation methods.
